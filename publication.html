Networked Control Systems

How does imperfect communication, such as channel fading, packet loss, delay and power constraints, limit what a networked control system can achieve? We characterized the channel capacity required for mean-square stabilization of linear systems over power-constrained fading and finite-state Markov channels; showed that packet drops make the number of components in the posterior of the optimal estimator grow exponentially, and designed tractable event-triggered estimators; and derived explicit consensusability conditions that couple agent dynamics, network topology and channel statistics. We also studied consensusability and distributed control of physically interconnected systems, and recently extended these results to output consensus of heterogeneous agents under Markov packet loss. Many of these results are collected in our Wiley-IEEE Press monograph.

Learning and Control

How can neural networks and data enter the control loop without giving up guarantees, and how can control theory make neural networks more reliable? Neural networks for control: structured neural controllers that are stable for any trainable parameters (for port-Hamiltonian, Lagrangian and multi-agent systems), learned neural contraction metrics and Lyapunov functions, and fast neural MPC with control barrier functions. Control for neural networks: Hamiltonian deep networks with provably non-vanishing gradients, contractive neural ODEs that are robust to adversarial inputs, and efficient verification of neural control barrier functions. Data-driven control and finite-sample analysis: robust controllers designed directly from input-output data, finite-sample guarantees for system identification and LQR design over lossy channels, global convergence of policy gradient under packet loss, and safety filters with provable performance bounds.

Robot Navigation and Manipulation

Our early work designed adaptive, output-feedback and robust controllers for robot manipulators and underactuated mechanical systems under kinematic, dynamic and actuator uncertainties. More recently we work towards embodied intelligence: robust visual odometry in dynamic and low-light scenes (BRIGHT-VO improves pose accuracy by about 25% in low light), and G-DRAGON, which grounds natural-language commands in OpenStreetMap with an on-board language model and completed person-search missions of up to 500 m on a real ground vehicle in unseen urban areas. For vision-language navigation, HAVANA is a dual-system video-action model in which a slow system produces a language-conditioned latent plan from the observation history and a fast system turns the latest plan and the current observation into local trajectories; the two run asynchronously, so no future video is generated at inference time, and the model achieves state-of-the-art results on OmniNavBench and generalizes to unseen real-world environments across different robots.
