Networked Control Systems

This line of research studies how imperfect communication, including channel fading, i.i.d. and Markovian packet loss, transmission delay, power constraints and cyber-attacks, limits the stabilization, consensus and estimation of networked control systems and multi-agent systems. For a single control loop closed over power-constrained fading channels and Gaussian finite-state Markov channels, we characterized the mean-square capacity of the channel and showed that mean-square stabilizability is determined by comparing this capacity with the instability of the plant, i.e., its unstable eigenvalues. For multi-agent systems, we derived consensusability conditions over analog fading networks, directed fading graphs, Markovian packet-loss channels, and channels with both delays and packet dropouts. These conditions reveal how the agent dynamics, the network topology (through the eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian) and the channel statistics jointly determine whether consensus can be reached. We further related the consensusability of interconnected multi-agent systems to the simultaneous stabilization problem, and showed that, in remote state estimation with stochastic event-triggered sensor schedules and packet drops, the optimal estimator has a Gaussian-mixture structure that admits tractable approximations. Recent work extends these ideas to output consensus of heterogeneous agents under Markov packet loss, and to resilient formation and fault-tolerant control of unmanned surface vessels under DoS and hybrid cyber-attacks. Many of these results are collected in our Wiley-IEEE Press monograph. Learning-based counterparts of these problems, such as finite-sample LQR design and policy-gradient methods over lossy channels, are listed under Learning and Control.

Learning and Control

This line of research asks how neural networks and data can be brought into control loops while keeping rigorous guarantees, and conversely how control theory can make neural networks better behaved and verifiable. Neural networks for control: we design structured neural network controllers whose architecture guarantees closed-loop properties for any choice of trainable parameters, so that training only improves performance and never breaks stability. Examples include neural energy-Casimir control of port-Hamiltonian systems, structured deep neural network backstepping controllers for Lagrangian systems with explicit tracking-error bounds (also when the model is learned from data) and their distributed extension to formation control of multi-agent Lagrangian systems with explicit formation-error bounds, jointly learned neural contraction metrics and controllers for exponential stabilization, neural Lyapunov control of interconnected systems with unknown dynamics whose structured Lyapunov functions are certified by an SMT solver, and neural MPC with control barrier functions that runs about 200 times faster than standard MPC on embedded hardware and adapts to parameter changes without retraining. Control for neural networks: viewing deep networks as discretized dynamical systems, we proposed Hamiltonian deep neural networks that provably avoid vanishing gradients regardless of depth, and contractive (convolutional) neural ODEs whose robustness to input noise and adversarial attacks is promoted by simple weight regularization. We also developed tighter Jacobian bounds for formally verifying neural control barrier functions with smooth activations such as tanh, which substantially raise verification success rates and scalability. Data-driven control and finite-sample analysis: we designed worst-case robust tracking controllers directly from input-output data via convex programs, developed finite-sample tests of spectral radius and stabilizability for networked control systems, derived finite-sample guarantees for identifying linear systems under random packet loss, and proposed curvature-guided safety filters with provable bounds on the performance loss relative to the optimal safe action. For LQR over channels with unknown packet loss, we showed how the error of a loss rate estimated from finite channel samples determines closed-loop stability and suboptimality, giving explicit sample-complexity bounds; and we proved that policy gradient methods still converge to the global optimum despite non-convexity, with a tailored Gauss-Newton method converging exponentially fast at the same rate as in the loss-free case once the step size is scaled by the loss rate.

Robot Navigation and Manipulation

This line of research aims at reliable control, perception and navigation for autonomous robots. Our early work developed adaptive backstepping, output-feedback and robust L2-gain controllers for robot manipulators and underactuated mechanical systems, guaranteeing trajectory tracking and stabilization in the presence of kinematic, dynamic and actuator uncertainties. More recently we have moved towards embodied intelligence, starting with robust visual odometry in challenging scenes. For dynamic urban environments, we combine learned feature matching with semantic segmentation, discarding features on movable objects and extracting line features from static man-made structures, and suppress dynamic objects through adaptive masked optical-flow refinement. BRIGHT-VO is a brightness-guided Transformer visual odometry that fuses IMU measurements through pose-graph optimization; it improves pose accuracy by about 20% in normal outdoor conditions and about 25% in low light. G-DRAGON lets a robot follow natural-language commands outdoors by using a locally deployed large language model to ground them in OpenStreetMap, combined with global route planning, SLAM and open-vocabulary semantic exploration; on a real unmanned ground vehicle it completed person-search missions with trajectories of up to 500 m in unseen urban areas.
